  BRANCH MERGE rather than a PR, so nothing checked it on the way in.

- **THE BIGGEST FINDING OF THE WHOLE INCIDENT WAS NOT THE DDL STORM.**
  `pregame_line_poller.last_known_prices()` rebuilds a fingerprint map on EVERY
  tick of a 24x7 loop, re-reading DK's entire pre-game history for every
  unstarted game — ~1,525 games, ~142k heap fetches out of a 1.18 GB table.
  From `pg_stat_statements`: **4,291 calls, 88,398 s total, 20,601 ms mean —
  24.6 HOURS of database time**, more than DOUBLE the 11.6 hours the DDL storm
  cost. At a 30-second interval and a 20.6 s mean the loop spent ~69% of every
  cycle in this one query, and at the >60 s tail (measured: a bare
  `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` timed out at 60 s) it ran back to back and slept not at all.

- **The plan was never the problem, which is why an index was the wrong first
  instinct.** `EXPLAIN` shows a good plan: drive from `games` (1,525 unstarted),
  nested-loop into `odds` on `idx_odds_book_snap`. Nothing is missing. The cost
  is 142k random heap fetches, repeated every 30 seconds for no reason —
  **the poller already knows what it wrote.**

- Fixed by making the map caller-owned: `run_forever` seeds it once, `poll_once`
  folds its own writes in AFTER the commit (before, and a failed insert leaves
  the map claiming a price the database never took — the next tick then sees no
  change and never retries it), and a bounded `PREGAME_POLL_RESEED_SEC` (900s)
  re-seeds so another writer's move and started-game dead weight cannot drift
  forever. A tick that dies throws the map away rather than carrying a guess.
  **2,880 rebuilds a day become 96.**

- **`snapshot_at` is TEXT**, so `snapshot_at::timestamptz` is a non-immutable
  cast and CANNOT be indexed — worth knowing before anyone reaches for the
  obvious expression index. A covering partial index is still the right
  belt-and-braces follow-up, but it is DDL on the hottest table and belongs in
  a quiet window, not at 7pm ET with live games running.

- **Watched fail, not assumed:** always-rebuild fails 2; writes not folded fails
  2; folding before the commit fails 1; a failed tick keeping its map fails 1.
  Suite **2,032 passed**.

**Session summary (2026-09-01, session 167 — the Stats page error was the whole API, and the cause was our own DDL):**

- Matt: *"Still having this error on the stats page and now no players are
  showing"* — two screenshots, NCAAF Pass Yards and MLB Hits, both showing
  `Connection error: [object Object]` above `No players`.

- **IT WAS NOT THE STATS PAGE, AND NOT THE DATA.** The RPCs behind the board are
  healthy: `player_recent_games_mlb(2026,'batter',5)` returns **3,197 rows in
  780 ms**, and it returns them as `anon`. `player_recent_games_ncaaf(2026,5)`
  returns 686. Grants are correct, the functions exist. The board was empty
  because **PostgREST was answering 503 to every request**. `edge_logs` for the
  minute in Matt's screenshot (10:11 UTC = 6:11 ET, the clock on the phone):
  **215 responses, all 503.** Every logged call to those two RPCs in 24h was a
  500 or a 503; not one succeeded.

- **THE CAUSE IS THIS REPO'S OWN write-time DDL.** Seven modules create their
  table at write time and re-ran the whole CREATE/ALTER/REVOKE block on **every
  call**, on the assumption that `IF NOT EXISTS` makes it free. Postgres
  disagrees on three counts: `CREATE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS` takes a SHARE lock
  before it discovers the index exists, `ALTER TABLE ... ENABLE ROW LEVEL
  SECURITY` takes ACCESS EXCLUSIVE whether or not RLS is on, and **each one
  fires Supabase's `pgrst_ddl_watch` event trigger — PostgREST then returns 503
  to the entire app until its schema cache rebuilds.** From
  `pg_stat_statements` (measured, not estimated):

  | statement | calls | mean | total |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `CREATE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS idx_api_call_ts` | 1,925 | 15,082 ms | 29,032 s |
  | `ALTER TABLE api_call_log ENABLE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` | 1,676 | 7,797 ms | 13,067 s |

  **11.6 hours of database time re-creating a table that already existed**, and
  ~3,600 forced cache reloads. `postgres_logs` shows the reload query itself —
  PostgREST's giant catalog `WITH base_types AS ...` — timing out **232 times**
  against the 8s `authenticator` limit, plus 108 timeouts of the `ALTER TABLE`
  and 30 each on `v_latest_odds_all_books` / `v_latest_dk_odds` reads starved
  behind it.

- **It is self-reinforcing, which is why it got worse rather than settling.**
  `monitoring/probe.py`'s writer drops its connection on ANY failure and clears
  its `ensured` flag, so it re-runs the ensure block on reconnect. The busier
  the database got, the more DDL it fired at it, the longer the cache stayed
  down.

  remove redundant work. Uses `%s` placeholders and a cursor fallback so it
