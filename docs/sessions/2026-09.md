**Session summary (2026-09-03, session 182 — the tuner was validating on shuffled folds, and walk-forward says f5 did not fail, it BROKE):**

- mike: *"why holdout an entire season? why not hold out various intervals of all
  seasons?"* → *"Do it."*
- **RANDOM INTERVALS WOULD BE WORSE, and the season holdout was never the weak
  part.** Every feature here is a rolling window (`d_runs_last_5`,
  `d_runs_last_10`, `d_starter_era_last3`), so a held-out June game is described
  by May games that sit in the training set. A season holdout avoids that by
  construction and matches deployment.
- **The weak part was the TUNER.** `_xgb_objective` and
  `_xgb_multiclass_objective` used `StratifiedKFold(shuffle=True)` — randomly
  shuffled folds — while the final holdout was a clean season. So 100 Optuna
  trials optimised a different task: interpolating between games already seen.
  Replaced with `TimeSeriesSplit` (`_time_ordered_cv`), and `train_model` now
  sorts the frame by `game_date` before building the arrays, because the split
  is positional and an unsorted frame makes it an arbitrary partition wearing a
  better name.
- **Measured, and smaller than I claimed.** I said fixing the CV was probably
  worth more than any feature. It was not, for this model: best CV log-loss
  0.6503 (shuffled) → 0.6525 (honest), and the 2026 holdout barely moved
  (AUC 0.568 → 0.569, accuracy 0.560 → 0.561). The leak was real but small here.
  The fix is still right — the tuner now optimises the task the model is for —
  but it did not rescue anything.
- **THE WALK-FORWARD IS THE FINDING.** `scripts/walk_forward_eval.py` trains
  ≤T and tests T+1, walked across seasons, fixed params so the only moving part
  is the season. `mlb_f5_moneyline`:

  | test season | train n | test n | acc | AUC |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 2022 | 3,593 | 1,481 | 0.5841 | **0.6362** |
  | 2023 | 5,074 | 1,530 | 0.5902 | **0.6283** |
  | 2024 | 6,604 | 1,470 | 0.6000 | **0.6396** |
  | 2025 | 8,074 | 1,303 | 0.5848 | **0.6333** |
  | 2026 | 9,377 | 1,058 | 0.5529 | **0.5598** |

- **Four seasons at AUC ≈ 0.63, then a collapse to 0.56 in 2026.** The single
  2026 holdout said "this model has no signal" and I was about to recommend
  retiring it on that basis. Five folds say **"this model worked for four
  seasons and broke in 2026"** — a different problem with a different fix, and
  exactly the distinction one holdout cannot make. This is §7's plateau rule
  applied to the model rather than to a threshold.
- **So the f5 recommendation changes: do not retire it. Find out what broke in
  2026.** Candidates to check next: a feature source that stopped populating or
  changed shape this season, the 17.6% null-feature drop rate, and whether the
  break is gradual or steps at a date.
- Three mutations on the CV change and five tests on the harness. Suite: 2,232
  passed, 1 failed (pre-existing).

- mike: *"sweep the f5 min_edge and add the first pitch guard."*
- **THE SWEEP FOUND A BUG BEFORE IT FOUND AN ANSWER.**
  `calibrated_threshold_sweep._apply_price_floor` read

  the 52 models that never named a floor. So the sweep was choosing cuts on a
  population the scorer now refuses, which is the exact bug that function's own
  docstring describes one level down. Fixed to `config.min_odds_for`. Same fix
  in `scripts/emit_threshold_sql.py`, which generates the APP's action filter —
  left alone, the app would have shown picks the scorer declines to bet.
- **The fix changed the answer completely.** `mlb_f5_moneyline` at its live cut
  went from **23-11, +2.7%** to **8-9, −18.5%** once only placeable bets count.
  Most of its apparent record was bets at prices we no longer take.
- **No min_edge works.** Holding `min_prob` at 0.74, every value from 0.00 to
  0.12 is negative (−8.9% to −18.5%), all under MIN_SETTLED, both halves
  negative (−3.8% / −33.3%). And 0.00 through 0.08 are IDENTICAL at 18 bets —
  at that probability every surviving pick already has edge ≥ 0.08, so
  **min_edge is not a lever for this model at all**. The sub-1% edges only ever
  occurred on heavy favourites, which the −200 floor already removes.
- The harness's own recommendation is 0.58/0.10 (82-58, +7.9%, plateau 8/8) —
  but that abandons the probability cut entirely, quadruples volume to
  12.1/week, and its second half is **+0.2%**. Not shipped; §7 says report the
  neighbourhood and retrain rather than ship the least-bad cut.
- **THE FIRST-PITCH GUARD IS TWO GUARDS, because the data showed two failures.**
  507 signals were locked after first pitch: **485 had their PICK created after
  first pitch too** (never pre-game picks, §7 — capture should never have
  locked them), and 22 were legitimate picks whose CAPTURE merely ran late. The
  MIN/DET signal that held the health check red was one of the 22 — created
  **38 seconds before** first pitch, captured four minutes after.
- So capture is keyed on `p.created_at` (not the capture clock, which would
  delete the 22 legitimate signals from the shadow record — §1c), and delivery
  is keyed on whether the game has started by the time the card is built
  (posting one of the 22 sends a member to a game in progress at a pre-game
  number). Both fail OPEN on a missing timestamp.
- The delivery half is in Python, not SQL: these producers are executed against
  sqlite by the tests, where `::timestamptz` is a syntax error — the same lesson

- `_still_pre_game` initially raised `NameError: timezone` — `discord_notifier`

- The restatement path is deliberately NOT guarded: it re-publishes a whole past

  tick of a 24x7 loop, re-reading DK's entire pre-game history for every
  unstarted game — ~1,525 games, ~142k heap fetches out of a 1.18 GB table.
  From `pg_stat_statements`: **4,291 calls, 88,398 s total, 20,601 ms mean —
  24.6 HOURS of database time**, more than DOUBLE the 11.6 hours the DDL storm
  cost. At a 30-second interval and a 20.6 s mean the loop spent ~69% of every
  cycle in this one query, and at the >60 s tail (measured: a bare
  `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` timed out at 60 s) it ran back to back and slept not at all.

- **The plan was never the problem, which is why an index was the wrong first
  instinct.** `EXPLAIN` shows a good plan: drive from `games` (1,525 unstarted),
  nested-loop into `odds` on `idx_odds_book_snap`. Nothing is missing. The cost
  is 142k random heap fetches, repeated every 30 seconds for no reason —
  **the poller already knows what it wrote.**

- Fixed by making the map caller-owned: `run_forever` seeds it once, `poll_once`
  folds its own writes in AFTER the commit (before, and a failed insert leaves
  the map claiming a price the database never took — the next tick then sees no
  change and never retries it), and a bounded `PREGAME_POLL_RESEED_SEC` (900s)
  re-seeds so another writer's move and started-game dead weight cannot drift
  forever. A tick that dies throws the map away rather than carrying a guess.
  **2,880 rebuilds a day become 96.**

- **`snapshot_at` is TEXT**, so `snapshot_at::timestamptz` is a non-immutable
  cast and CANNOT be indexed — worth knowing before anyone reaches for the
  obvious expression index. A covering partial index is still the right
  belt-and-braces follow-up, but it is DDL on the hottest table and belongs in
  a quiet window, not at 7pm ET with live games running.

- **Watched fail, not assumed:** always-rebuild fails 2; writes not folded fails
  2; folding before the commit fails 1; a failed tick keeping its map fails 1.
