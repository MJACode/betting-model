## 2026-11-04 — NCAAB first-half CLV uses a later pre-tip row

**Why.** NCAAB first-half picks (`ncaab_first_half_total`, `ncaab_first_half_spread`, markets `totals_h1` / `spreads_h1`) were stamped `standard_v2` with every closing field empty. The 2026-11-03 NCAAB beat-close line was 1/1 with 13 picks. `tracking/paper_tracker._closing_odds` accepted only `snapshot_type = 'close'` for those markets, the rule copied from the NBA first-half and team-total feeds. `data/ingestors/ncaab_first_half_odds.py` never writes a close row; every pull is `open`.

**What changed.** For NCAAB `totals_h1` / `spreads_h1`, the close is the bet book's newest pre-tip row on the same line pulled strictly after the pick was created. The decision pull is not the close. No such row leaves the pick unmeasured. NBA first half and team totals still read `close` rows only. The measurement write goes through `_real_close`: a null close does not set `standard_v2`. The migration's empty stamp skips these two models, and a started game with no post-creation row is left out of the recompute and the Python backfill scan so it does not pin the queue. `scripts/recompute_empty_clv.py` lists picks already stamped `standard_v2` with an empty close. It is a dry run until `--apply`, and it takes `--date` and `--sport`. It was not run.

**Verified.** `tests/test_ncaab_first_half_clv.py`: a same-book pull 10 minutes before tip, after the pick was created, is the total's close; a spread with no pull after its creation is not updated; an NBA first half ignores a later `open` row and reads the `close` row; a null close, including an older method on an NCAAB first half, is not stamped. Related CLV tests passed (137).

**Not done.** The script was not run against production. The six-hour empty stamp remains for other markets, and an NBA bet with no league tip is still stamped empty after two days, so those queues still drain. No threshold, stake, pause, record, or recap-format change.

**How a table converges.** It is stored as chunks, each covering a range of primary keys, each with its row count and
a fingerprint of its rows computed by Postgres.
- Tables under 2M rows, the 14 tables with no primary key, `latest_odds` and `latest_prop_odds` are copied whole every
  run. The two `latest_*` tables were measured that day at 16.8M updates and 82M deletes against 2.8M and 8.8M live
  rows.
- The seven bigger tables are caught up: new keys are appended, every range's count is compared (deletes), and the
  newest 40 ranges plus the oldest-checked seventh have their fingerprints compared (edits).
- None of those seven has an `updated_at` column (measured from the catalog), so the fingerprint is the only way an
  edit is found.

**Load rules, as built.**
- Every read is one transaction on the transaction pooler: `BEGIN ... READ ONLY` with `SET LOCAL statement_timeout =
  60s`, UTC, and pinned date and float output, so a fingerprint means the same thing on every connection.
- `SET SESSION` was not used. On the transaction pooler it would stay on a shared server connection, and a live scorer
  could inherit read-only.
- At most 50k rows or about 32 MB a read, then 0.5 s of rest.
- It waits while more than 6 other client queries are active, checked at most every 15 s.
- No reads at 10:00-10:50 UTC or at :15-:27 of hours 11-22 UTC. A read does not start within 75 s of either window.
